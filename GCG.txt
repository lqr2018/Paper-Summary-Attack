###Introduction:
discusses the current efforts to align large language models (LLMs) to prevent the generation of harmful content. Despite these alignment mechanisms, the models can still be bypassed, especially through manually designed "jailbreak" attacks. ### Related Work:
 
1.Adversarial Examples & Transferability:

The paper builds on a rich body of research on adversarial examples, where small perturbations to inputs can lead to drastic changes in model output. This concept was first explored in computer vision and has since been extended to other domains like natural language processing.
Prior work has focused on adversarial examples for image classification (e.g., Szegedy et al., 2014) and methods to defend against such attacks (e.g., Madry et al., 2018). However, creating reliable adversarial attacks for language models has proven more challenging due to the discrete nature of text inputs.
Previous studies such as AutoPrompt (Shin et al., 2020) and PEZ (Wen et al., 2023) explored optimizing prompts for adversarial text attacks but with limited success on aligned models.
Alignment of Language Models:

2.The alignment of language models with human values, typically through fine-tuning with human feedback, is a key area of research. Papers like Ouyang et al. (2022) and Bai et al. (2022) discuss methods of aligning LLMs to avoid harmful content generation.
Despite these alignment efforts, jailbreaks—manual or automated attacks to bypass safety mechanisms—remain a significant challenge for LLMs (e.g., Wei et al., 2023). This paper contributes by automating these attacks and improving their success rates and transferability.
